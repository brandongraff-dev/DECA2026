"So where do we start? With ten old rocket stages from the US, India and France. We picked them for two reasons. First, they sit in the orbit band where cheap, shared SpaceX launches already go, so our truck makes its first pickup just eighty days after launch. Second, no permission problem. Under space law, every object still belongs to the country that launched it. These belong to countries whose space agencies already pay for cleanup. The bigger prize comes later: a hundred forty-two Soviet-era rocket stages. But Russia must agree, and getting there takes a year of thrusting, so those cost about thirteen million each." Q&A backup: The stages: 4 Indian PSLV, 4 French Ariane and 2 US (Agena, Delta) upper stages, 600–1,900 kg each, in the sun-synchronous band at 650–850 km. Legal basis: Outer Space Treaty, Article VIII (state of registry keeps jurisdiction and control).
