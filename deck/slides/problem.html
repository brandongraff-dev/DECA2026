"This is low Earth orbit in our simulation, using this October's real catalogue. The European Space Agency tracks about forty-seven thousand objects; only about sixteen thousand still work. ESA's own conclusion this year: even with no new launches, debris keeps growing, because collisions create pieces faster than they fall. That's the Kessler syndrome. But the risk is concentrated. A 2021 study found that removing just the fifty most dangerous derelicts, mostly old rocket stages, would cut their collision risk roughly in half." Q&A backup: Why not just let them fall? Above about 800 km, dead objects can stay up for a century or more. The SL-8 stages we target sit near 960 km.
