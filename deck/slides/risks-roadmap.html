V · Solution · Risks and roadmap

What could stop us, and what we do about it.

Capture

Grabbing a tumbling stage hasn’t been done yet. We follow ADRAS-J2 and partner rather than invent the arm.

Cost

Truck and kit prices are estimates. We test them with experts and keep a 10% fuel margin.

Legal consent

Owners keep control of their objects. We start with customers’ own and partner-nation objects.

Re-entry safety

Heavy objects get a controlled re-entry over the ocean, not a slow fall.

"Our four biggest risks. Capture: nobody has grabbed a tumbling rocket stage yet, so we watch Japan's ADRAS-J2 and partner with whoever proves it. Cost: our prices are estimates, so I'm testing them with experts. Legal: owners keep control of their objects, so we start with customers' own and partner-nation objects. Safety: heavy objects get a controlled re-entry over the ocean. The roadmap: validate this year, sign a capture partner, fly a small pathfinder, then our first ten-stop truck from 2029." Q&A backup: Why not build the capture system ourselves? It's the hardest, least-proven part, and two funded companies (Astroscale, ClearSpace) are already working on it. Our edge is the route and the pricing, not the arm. Dates after 2027 are targets, not commitments.
