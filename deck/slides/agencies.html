III · Customer Segments · Agencies (Product B)

Our first route starts where the cheap rockets already go.

26 rocket stages owned by the US, India and France sit in the sun-synchronous band, 650–850 km up. Rideshare launches drop satellites in that band, so our truck reaches its first stop in 80 days.

Why agencies: they already pay for removal, at milestones, and their own countries own these stages.

The bigger prize, later: 142 Soviet SL-8 stages at 83°. They need Russia’s consent and a year-long climb, so they cost more: $13.0M each.

"Product B is for agencies. Each dot here is an object we downloaded. The dead ones, in orange, line up in tight clusters, and clusters are routes. Our first route is in the sun-synchronous band. That matters because the cheap shared rockets, rideshares, drop satellites right there. Twenty-six old stages there belong to the US, India and France, the same countries whose agencies already pay for removal. Our truck reaches its first stop eighty days after launch. The bigger prize is the SL-8 cluster: a hundred forty-two Soviet stages. But they belong to Russia, which must consent, and getting there takes a year of thrusting to change our orbit's tilt. So that comes later, at about thirteen million each." Q&A backup: Legal basis: Outer Space Treaty (1967), Article VIII: the state of registry keeps jurisdiction and control over its objects. First-route stages: 4 Indian PSLV, 4 French Ariane and 2 US (Agena, Delta) upper stages, 600–1,900 kg each.
