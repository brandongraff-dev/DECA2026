III · Customer Segments

Who pays to take out the trash?

1

Space agencies

ESA · Japan · UK · US

Why they care

Their old rockets are up there, and they already pay for cleanup.

How they buy

Contracts, paid at milestones.

2

Satellite companies

Iridium · OneWeb · Planet

Why they care

Must bring dead satellites down within 5 years. Some will fail.

How they buy

A fixed fee per satellite removed.

3

Insurers

Space underwriters

Why they care

They pay out when satellites are lost or hit.

How they buy

Removal bundled with policies, once it's proven.

“I expect the cost is really in the millions or tens of millions, at which price I know it doesn't make sense.” — Matt Desch, CEO of Iridium (2019), on removing its dead satellites

"Who pays to take out the trash? Three customers, in the order they'll pay. First, space agencies: Europe, Japan, the UK and the US. Their old rockets are up there, and they're already signing cleanup contracts. Second, satellite companies like Iridium and OneWeb. They must bring dead satellites down within five years, and some will fail. But listen to Iridium's CEO: at millions per satellite, he said, it doesn't make sense. So companies need a much lower price, which is exactly what a route gives. Third, insurers, who pay out when satellites are lost. They'll join once removal is proven." Q&A backup: Why agencies first? They have budgets now. Companies own their satellites, which makes them legally simple, but they're price-sensitive, so they're early design partners. 24 dead first-generation Iridium satellites are still in orbit (Oct 2026 data); a 10-stop route through them models ≈$12.5M each. Rocket Lab agreed to buy Iridium in June 2026 (≈$8B).
