Our model · J2 nodal precession (Vallado, 2013)

"So how can one truck reach ten objects cheaply? Here's the secret: we don't chase the junk. We let Earth bring it to us. Every orbit slowly swings around Earth, like the hand of a clock. And lower orbits swing faster, because Earth isn't a perfect ball; it bulges at the equator. So our truck parks a little higher or lower than its next target, and simply waits until the two line up, like the hands of a clock meeting. The difference is huge. Turning with fuel to close one of these gaps would take about a thousand kilograms of propellant. Waiting a year takes about nineteen." Q&A backup: The swing is called nodal precession, caused by Earth's oblateness (the J2 term): dΩ/dt = −1.5·n·J2·(R/p)²·cos i. Our planner prices waiting time against fuel, so routes aren't just fuel-optimal; they're cost-optimal.
