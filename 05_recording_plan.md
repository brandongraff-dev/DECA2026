# Backup demo video (if the live demo glitches)

**The video is already made:** `video/OrbitalCurbside_demo.mp4` (2 min 35 s, 1280×720, silent). It is also inside the offline demo folder and the Windows zip. It plays in any video player with no internet.

It was rendered frame by frame from the offline demo itself, so it shows exactly what the live demo shows. To re-make it after changing the sim or the truck model, run `python video/record_video.py` (about 80 minutes).

**In the presentation:** if the live demo doesn't start within 20 seconds, say "Let me show you the recording" and play the MP4. Don't troubleshoot in front of the judge; setup time counts toward your 15 minutes. Copy the file to the laptop's desktop **and** a USB stick.

## What's in it, and what to say over it

The video is silent so you can talk over it live. Read this at a calm pace (about 290 words). You can also record it as a voice-over: on Windows, open the MP4 in **Clipchamp** (free, built in) and use **Record > Audio**; on a Mac, use **iMovie > Record Voiceover**.

| Time | On screen | Say |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:07 | Title, then card "1 of 5" | "Here's Orbital Curbside, running on real orbits." |
| 0:07–0:18 | Scene 1: every tracked object in low orbit, globe turning | "This is low Earth orbit from this October's public catalogue: about nineteen thousand objects. The grey dots still work. The red ones are pieces from past crashes, and the gold ones are dead satellites and old rocket stages. Nobody is coming to collect them." |
| 0:18–0:34 | Card "2 of 5", then scene 2: three blue rings fanning apart | "Earth bulges at the equator, so every orbit slowly swings around it, and lower orbits swing faster. These three rings start lined up and drift apart on their own. Turning an orbit with a rocket costs a lot of fuel. Waiting for Earth to do it is almost free." |
| 0:34–1:01 | Card "3 of 5", then scene 3: the orange truck's route | "This is our first route: ten old rocket stages from the US, India and France. The orange truck parks at a slightly different height, waits until the next stage's orbit lines up, then hops over and attaches a small rocket kit that sends the stage down to burn up. Ten stops in about sixteen months from launch, on about two hundred kilograms of xenon." |
| 1:01–1:12 | Card "4 of 5", then scene 4: the cost bars | "Same job, two ways. One mission per piece costs about ninety-seven million dollars each. One truck for ten costs about twelve million each, launch included." |
| 1:12–2:30 | Card "5 of 5", then the 3D truck journey | "Here's one pickup up close. The truck rides a shared SpaceX rocket, unfolds its solar wings and spends eighty days climbing to the first stage. Its arm grabs the stage, stops the spin and latches on a kit. The truck backs away, the kit fires, and weeks later the stage burns up in the atmosphere. Then the truck drives on to the next stop." |
| 2:30–2:35 | End card: $12.2M per piece | "Space cleanup, priced like a garbage route." |

Numbers on screen come from our model and the 16 Oct 2026 catalogue. The sim's panel shows **135 kg** of xenon for the route itself; the **198 kg** in the deck also counts the 80-day climb after launch. "About two hundred kilograms" covers both.

## If you'd rather record your own screen

Use **Snipping Tool > Record** (Windows 11) or **Xbox Game Bar** (`Win + Alt + R`). Run `Start Demo.bat`, press `F11` for full screen, close notifications, then follow the table above with keys `1`–`4`. Rehearse the key presses three times and record two takes.
